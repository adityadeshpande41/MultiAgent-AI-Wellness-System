# Comprehensive Nutrition Knowledge Base

## Macronutrients Deep Dive

### Proteins
**Complete Proteins (contain all essential amino acids):**
- Animal sources: Chicken, beef, fish, eggs, dairy
- Plant sources: Quinoa, buckwheat, hemp seeds, chia seeds, spirulina

**Incomplete Proteins (missing one or more essential amino acids):**
- Legumes: Beans, lentils, peas (low in methionine)
- Grains: Rice, wheat, oats (low in lysine)
- Nuts and seeds: Almonds, sunflower seeds (varies by type)

**Protein Combining Examples:**
- Rice + beans = complete protein
- Hummus + whole grain pita = complete protein
- Peanut butter + whole grain bread = complete protein

**Daily Requirements:**
- Sedentary adults: 0.8g per kg body weight
- Active individuals: 1.2-1.6g per kg body weight
- Athletes/strength training: 1.6-2.2g per kg body weight
- Older adults (65+): 1.0-1.2g per kg body weight

### Carbohydrates
**Simple Carbohydrates:**
- **Natural**: Fruits (fructose), milk (lactose), honey
- **Refined**: Table sugar, candy, soda, pastries
- **Timing**: Best consumed around workouts for quick energy

**Complex Carbohydrates:**
- **Starches**: Potatoes, rice, pasta, bread, oats
- **Fiber**: Vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains
- **Benefits**: Sustained energy, blood sugar stability, satiety

**Glycemic Index Guidelines:**
- **Low GI (55 or less)**: Oats, quinoa, sweet potatoes, most vegetables
- **Medium GI (56-69)**: Brown rice, whole grain bread, bananas
- **High GI (70+)**: White bread, white rice, watermelon, dates

### Fats
**Saturated Fats (limit to <10% of calories):**
- Sources: Butter, coconut oil, red meat, full-fat dairy
- Effects: Can raise LDL cholesterol in some individuals

**Monounsaturated Fats (beneficial):**
- Sources: Olive oil, avocados, nuts, seeds
- Benefits: Heart health, inflammation reduction

**Polyunsaturated Fats:**
- **Omega-3**: Fatty fish, walnuts, flaxseeds, chia seeds
- **Omega-6**: Vegetable oils, nuts, seeds
- **Ratio**: Aim for 1:1 to 1:4 omega-3 to omega-6 ratio

**Trans Fats (avoid completely):**
- Sources: Partially hydrogenated oils, some margarines, fried foods
- Effects: Increase LDL, decrease HDL, promote inflammation

## Micronutrients and Deficiency Prevention

### Water-Soluble Vitamins
**Vitamin C:**
- Sources: Citrus fruits, berries, bell peppers, broccoli
- Functions: Immune support, collagen synthesis, antioxidant
- Daily needs: 75mg (women), 90mg (men)

**B-Complex Vitamins:**
- **B12**: Animal products, nutritional yeast, fortified foods
- **Folate**: Leafy greens, legumes, fortified grains
- **B6**: Poultry, fish, potatoes, chickpeas
- **Thiamine (B1)**: Whole grains, pork, legumes

### Fat-Soluble Vitamins
**Vitamin D:**
- Sources: Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified foods, supplements
- Functions: Bone health, immune function, mood regulation
- Daily needs: 600-800 IU (higher for deficient individuals)

**Vitamin A:**
- Sources: Orange vegetables, leafy greens, liver, dairy
- Functions: Vision, immune function, cell growth

**Vitamin K:**
- Sources: Leafy greens, broccoli, Brussels sprouts
- Functions: Blood clotting, bone metabolism

### Essential Minerals
**Iron:**
- **Heme iron**: Red meat, poultry, fish (better absorbed)
- **Non-heme iron**: Spinach, lentils, tofu, fortified cereals
- **Absorption enhancers**: Vitamin C, meat proteins
- **Inhibitors**: Calcium, tannins (tea/coffee), phytates

**Calcium:**
- Sources: Dairy, leafy greens, sardines, almonds, fortified foods
- Daily needs: 1000mg (adults), 1200mg (women 50+, men 70+)
- **Absorption**: Requires vitamin D, magnesium

**Magnesium:**
- Sources: Nuts, seeds, whole grains, leafy greens, dark chocolate
- Functions: Muscle/nerve function, energy metabolism, bone health

**Zinc:**
- Sources: Oysters, red meat, poultry, beans, nuts, seeds
- Functions: Immune function, wound healing, protein synthesis

## Meal Planning and Timing

### Pre-Workout Nutrition (1-3 hours before)
**Goals**: Fuel muscles, maintain blood sugar, minimize GI distress
- **Carbohydrates**: 1-4g per kg body weight
- **Protein**: 0.25g per kg body weight
- **Fat**: Minimal (slows digestion)
- **Examples**: Banana with almond butter, oatmeal with berries, Greek yogurt with granola

### Post-Workout Nutrition (within 2 hours)
**Goals**: Replenish glycogen, stimulate protein synthesis, rehydrate
- **Carbohydrates**: 1-1.2g per kg body weight
- **Protein**: 20-40g high-quality protein
- **Ratio**: 3:1 or 4:1 carbs to protein for endurance athletes
- **Examples**: Chocolate milk, protein smoothie with fruit, chicken and rice

### Intermittent Fasting Protocols
**16:8 Method:**
- Fast: 16 hours (including sleep)
- Eating window: 8 hours (e.g., 12pm-8pm)
- Benefits: May improve insulin sensitivity, promote fat loss

**5:2 Method:**
- Normal eating: 5 days per week
- Restricted calories: 2 non-consecutive days (500-600 calories)
- Benefits: May support weight loss, metabolic health

**Considerations:**
- Not suitable for everyone (pregnant women, diabetics, eating disorder history)
- May affect athletic performance if poorly timed
- Hydration remains important during fasting periods

## Special Dietary Approaches

### Mediterranean Diet
**Core Components:**
- High: Olive oil, fish, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts
- Moderate: Poultry, eggs, dairy
- Limited: Red meat, processed foods, refined sugars
- **Benefits**: Heart health, brain function, longevity

### Plant-Based Nutrition
**Protein Sources**: Legumes, quinoa, tofu, tempeh, nuts, seeds
**Key Nutrients to Monitor:**
- Vitamin B12: Supplement or fortified foods
- Iron: Combine with vitamin C sources
- Omega-3: Algae supplements, walnuts, flaxseeds
- Calcium: Fortified plant milks, tahini, leafy greens

### Ketogenic Diet
**Macronutrient Ratios:**
- Fat: 70-80% of calories
- Protein: 15-25% of calories
- Carbohydrates: 5-10% of calories (20-50g daily)

**Potential Benefits**: Rapid weight loss, improved insulin sensitivity
**Considerations**: Difficult to maintain, may affect athletic performance, requires medical supervision for some conditions

## Hydration Science

### Daily Fluid Needs
**General Guidelines:**
- Men: 3.7 liters (15.5 cups) total fluids daily
- Women: 2.7 liters (11.5 cups) total fluids daily
- Athletes: Additional 500-750ml per hour of exercise

### Electrolyte Balance
**Sodium**: 2300mg daily limit (1500mg for heart health)
- Sources: Table salt, processed foods, sports drinks
- Functions: Fluid balance, nerve transmission, muscle contraction

**Potassium**: 3500-4700mg daily
- Sources: Bananas, potatoes, spinach, beans, yogurt
- Functions: Blood pressure regulation, muscle function

**Hydration Indicators:**
- Urine color: Pale yellow indicates good hydration
- Thirst: Late indicator of dehydration
- Body weight: 2% loss indicates dehydration

## Food Safety and Preparation

### Safe Food Handling
**Temperature Guidelines:**
- Refrigerator: 40°F (4°C) or below
- Freezer: 0°F (-18°C) or below
- Danger zone: 40-140°F (4-60°C) - bacteria multiply rapidly

**Cooking Temperatures:**
- Poultry: 165°F (74°C)
- Ground meat: 160°F (71°C)
- Whole cuts of beef/pork: 145°F (63°C)
- Fish: 145°F (63°C)

### Meal Prep Strategies
**Batch Cooking:**
- Cook grains, proteins, and roasted vegetables in large quantities
- Store in portion-sized containers for easy assembly
- Shelf life: 3-4 days refrigerated, 2-3 months frozen

**Prep-Ahead Components:**
- Wash and chop vegetables
- Cook hard-boiled eggs
- Prepare overnight oats
- Make homemade dressings and sauces

## Supplements and Functional Foods

### Evidence-Based Supplements
**Creatine Monohydrate:**
- Dosage: 3-5g daily
- Benefits: Increased power output, muscle mass, recovery
- Best for: High-intensity, short-duration activities

**Whey Protein:**
- Dosage: 20-40g post-workout
- Benefits: Muscle protein synthesis, convenience
- Alternatives: Casein, plant-based proteins

**Omega-3 Fatty Acids:**
- Dosage: 1-3g EPA/DHA daily
- Benefits: Heart health, inflammation reduction, brain function
- Sources: Fish oil, algae oil (vegan option)

**Vitamin D3:**
- Dosage: 1000-4000 IU daily (based on blood levels)
- Benefits: Bone health, immune function, mood
- Testing: 25(OH)D blood test recommended

### Probiotics and Gut Health
**Beneficial Strains:**
- Lactobacillus acidophilus: Digestive health, immune support
- Bifidobacterium longum: Gut barrier function, inflammation reduction
- Saccharomyces boulardii: Antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention

**Prebiotic Foods:**
- Garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, bananas, oats
- Function: Feed beneficial gut bacteria

## Age-Specific Nutrition Needs

### Children and Adolescents
**Growth Requirements:**
- Higher protein needs per kg body weight
- Adequate calcium and vitamin D for bone development
- Iron needs increase during growth spurts
- Limit added sugars to <10% of calories

### Older Adults (65+)
**Changing Needs:**
- Increased protein requirements (1.0-1.2g/kg)
- Vitamin B12 absorption may decrease
- Adequate fiber for digestive health
- Hydration awareness (thirst sensation decreases)
- Nutrient-dense foods due to decreased appetite

### Pregnancy and Lactation
**Key Nutrients:**
- Folic acid: 600mcg daily (prevent neural tube defects)
- Iron: 27mg daily (increased blood volume)
- Calcium: 1000mg daily (fetal bone development)
- DHA: 200-300mg daily (brain development)
- **Avoid**: Alcohol, high-mercury fish, raw/undercooked foods
